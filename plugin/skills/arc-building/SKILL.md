# Building an Arc

An Arc is the DM's hidden plan: a title, a premise, the stakes, Acts in order and the intended resolution. Each Act has a name, a purpose and its Beats in order. A Beat is a turning point play may reach or abandon; it is never a script.

1. **Pace by the Seed's Length.** A one-shot is one Arc of a single Act. A short Campaign is one Arc. A medium Campaign is a few Arcs; plan this one as the first. A long Campaign is an open succession; plan this one so it can hand off to the next.
2. **Draw on the Party.** At least one Beat should touch a Character's backstory, bond or flaw. Later Arcs also draw on previous Arcs' resolutions and their abandoned Beats as dangling threads, and on any steer the Player gave.
3. **Write the premise and stakes** as what happens if the Party does nothing, and what they stand to win or lose.
4. **Shape the Acts:** a beginning that pulls the Party in, a middle that complicates it, an end that forces a choice. Give each Act a purpose a DM could check against play.
5. **Write Beats as situations, not outcomes.** "The Party learns who hired them" works whether they find out by charm, theft or accident. Give each a short title (it becomes the Beat's handle) and a one-line description.
6. **Leave room.** A few Beats per Act are enough; the DM revises pending Beats as play goes.
7. **Create it** with `create_arc`. It is refused while a current Arc exists: read the Arc, then conclude or retire it first.

The Arc never reaches the Player: not in the pitch, Narration, summaries or recaps.
